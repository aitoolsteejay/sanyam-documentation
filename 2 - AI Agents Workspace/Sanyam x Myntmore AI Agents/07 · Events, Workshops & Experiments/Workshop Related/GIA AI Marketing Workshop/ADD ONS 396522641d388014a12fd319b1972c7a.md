# ADD ONS

**Add-on A — "Prompt a Stone"**

*What it is in plain English:* You take a photo of a gemstone on your phone and describe it to an AI the way you'd describe it to a very smart assistant who has read every GIA textbook but has never held a stone. The AI gives you a structured first draft. You correct it with your expertise.

*Dummy Input:*

> Photo of a blue stone + prompt: "This is a loose gemstone I've photographed under daylight. Analyze the hue, saturation, and tone of the blue. Suggest likely origin based on color distribution. Note any visible inclusions or surface characteristics. Format this as a preliminary grading observation."
> 

*Dummy Output:*

> "The stone displays a medium-dark violetish-blue hue with high saturation and approximately 75% tone. Color distribution appears slightly uneven with a possible color zoning band visible near the girdle. Inclusion fingerprint suggests possible silk — fine rutile needles consistent with Kashmir or Ceylon origin. No visible surface-reaching fractures. Preliminary observation: natural sapphire, likely unheated, origin inconclusive pending microscopic examination."
> 

*What the gemologist does with it:* Corrects the tone estimate, confirms or disputes origin hypothesis, adds their loupe observations — and the report is 80% done before they've typed a word.

**Add-on B — "Design from a Stone"**

*What it is in plain English:* A client brings you a stone they own or want to buy. Instead of saying "take it to a jeweller," you pull out your laptop and show them five ways it could look as a finished piece — right there in the meeting. You become the first creative conversation, not just the verification step.

*Dummy Input:*

> "I have a 2.3ct oval green tourmaline, vivid medium green, clean stone. Show me design concepts for: 1) a cocktail ring in rose gold, 2) a pendant for everyday wear, 3) an Art Deco ring in white gold with diamond accents. Realistic renders, not cartoons."
> 

*Dummy Output:*

> Five rendered images — an elegant rose gold halo ring, a minimalist bezel pendant, an Art Deco east-west setting with baguette diamonds, a yellow gold vintage-style piece, and a modern tension setting. Client picks two they love and goes to a jeweller with a direction instead of a vague idea.
> 

*What the gemologist does with it:* Charges a consultation fee for the session. Refers the client to a jeweller with the renders attached. Gets a cut of the referral or simply builds the relationship.

**Add-on C — "Translate the Certificate"**

*What it is in plain English:* A GIA report says "VS2, G, Excellent cut, no fluorescence." To a bride that means nothing. This add-on teaches you to feed that report into an AI and get back language that makes the stone feel worth what it costs.

*Dummy Input:*

> "Here is a GIA grading report: Round brilliant, 1.02ct, G color, VS2 clarity, Excellent cut, Excellent polish, Excellent symmetry, no fluorescence. Write a 3-paragraph description for a customer who knows nothing about diamonds but wants to feel confident they're buying something special."
> 

*Dummy Output:*

> "This diamond sits in the top 15% of all diamonds for color — white enough that the human eye cannot detect any warmth even in direct sunlight. Its clarity is VS2, meaning the tiny natural characteristics inside it are invisible without a 10x magnifying loupe and completely invisible to the naked eye. The cut grade of Excellent is the highest GIA awards — less than 3% of diamonds earn it — and it is this cut that determines how much light the diamond throws back at you when it catches the sun. This is a stone that will look as alive in 40 years as it does today."
> 

*What the gemologist does with it:* Uses this in their listing, sends it to the client alongside the certificate, or hands it to the jeweller they work with to use in their sales conversation.

**Add-on D — "The Trust Post"**

*What it is in plain English:* Most GIA gemologists have credentials that jewellers and wealthy buyers would pay to access — but they're invisible on LinkedIn because they don't know what to post. This add-on shows you how to turn your daily work into a consistent stream of content that makes the right people come to you.

*Dummy Input:*

> "I'm a GIA-certified gemologist in Mumbai. Write me a LinkedIn post explaining the difference between a natural and lab-grown diamond in a way that helps a jeweller answer a customer question confidently. Make it educational, not salesy. Under 200 words."
> 

*Dummy Output:*

> "A question I get from jewellers every week: 'How do I explain lab-grown diamonds to a customer without losing the sale?'
> 
> 
> Here's what I tell them.
> 
> A lab-grown diamond is chemically identical to a natural one. Same hardness. Same sparkle. Same GIA grading standards. The difference is origin and, increasingly, price — lab-grown diamonds are now 60–80% cheaper than natural.
> 
> What that means for the customer depends entirely on why they're buying.
> 
> If they want a symbol of something that took billions of years to form — natural.
> 
> If they want the biggest, most beautiful stone for their budget — lab-grown.
> 
> Neither answer is wrong. Your job as a jeweller isn't to push one over the other. It's to understand which customer is sitting in front of you.
> 
> That's what a GIA gemologist helps you figure out.
> 
> —
> 
> GIA Graduate Gemologist | Mumbai | DM if you want a free consultation framework for your team."
> 

*What the gemologist does with it:* Posts this once a week, builds a following of jewellers and buyers, gets inbound enquiries instead of cold calling.

**Add-on E — "Your AI Appraisal Assistant"**

*What it is in plain English:* An appraisal has two parts — the physical examination only you can do, and the paperwork, research, and report writing that takes just as long. This add-on automates the second half so you can do twice the volume without working twice the hours.

*Dummy Input (after physical exam):*

> "I've examined a 1.5ct cushion cut ruby. Pigeon blood red, moderate silk visible under microscope, no indications of heat treatment, origin likely Mozambique based on inclusion fingerprint. Current market comp range I found: $4,200–$6,800 per carat for unheated Mozambique ruby of this quality. Write a formal appraisal narrative for insurance purposes."
> 

*Dummy Output:*

> "GEMOLOGICAL APPRAISAL REPORT
> 
> 
> Stone: Natural Ruby | Shape: Cushion Brilliant | Weight: 1.50ct
> 
> Color: Vivid red with slight purplish secondary hue, consistent with 'pigeon blood' designation | Clarity: Moderate silk inclusions, characteristic of natural unheated corundum | Treatment: No indications of heat treatment detected under gemological examination | Origin: Mozambique (probable, based on inclusion characteristics)
> 
> Replacement Value: INR 7,40,000 – 8,60,000 (based on current market comparables for unheated Mozambique ruby, 1.50ct, VS clarity equivalent, as of [date])
> 
> This appraisal is prepared for insurance replacement purposes only and does not constitute an offer to purchase."
> 

*What the gemologist does with it:* Reviews, adjusts the value range based on their judgment, signs it. A report that used to take 45 minutes now takes 12.

**Add-on F — "Natural vs Lab: The Conversation"**

*What it is in plain English:* Lab-grown diamonds are the most confusing topic in the industry right now. Different people need to hear different things — a bride, a retailer, and an investor all have completely different stakes. This add-on shows you how to use AI to build a tailored explainer for each, so you're never caught fumbling the conversation.

*Dummy Input:*

> "My client is a 28-year-old bride with a budget of Rs 3 lakhs. Her fiancé wants to buy a 1ct diamond solitaire. She's heard about lab-grown diamonds and asked me if they're 'fake.' Write me a calm, honest explanation I can give her in a WhatsApp message."
> 

*Dummy Output:*

> "Lab-grown diamonds are 100% real diamonds — the same atoms, the same hardness, the same sparkle as one that came from the earth. The only difference is where they were born: a mine vs a laboratory. Think of it like ice from a glacier vs ice from your freezer. Same water. Same ice. Different origin story.
> 
> 
> The reason people ask about them now is price — a lab-grown 1ct diamond costs 60–70% less than a natural one. That means with your budget, you could get a significantly larger or higher quality stone.
> 
> The question worth asking yourself: is the origin part of what makes it meaningful to you? For some people it is. For others, the stone on their finger is what matters.
> 
> There's no wrong answer. I can show you both options and we can decide together."
> 

*What the gemologist does with it:* Sends this as-is or lightly edits it. Builds versions for retailers, for investors, for parents buying a gift. Has a ready response for every scenario instead of improvising.
