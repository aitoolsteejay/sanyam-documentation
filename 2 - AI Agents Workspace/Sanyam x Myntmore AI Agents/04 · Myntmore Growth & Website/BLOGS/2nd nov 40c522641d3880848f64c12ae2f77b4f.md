34. The Target List Trap: Why Account-Based Marketing Beats Spraying Your Whole ICP

URL: [https://www.myntmore.com/blog/account-based-marketing-b2b](https://www.myntmore.com/blog/account-based-marketing-b2b)

Some deals are worth 100 times more than others. So why does your outbound treat a ₹4 crore enterprise account exactly like a five-person startup?

### Volume Thinking Breaks at the Top of the Market

Most outbound is built for volume. Define the ICP, build a large list, run sequences, and count the meetings. That works well when deals are small and decisions are simple.

It breaks down when you’re chasing high-value accounts. An enterprise buyer doesn’t make a decision because one person opened one email. Your best-fit accounts typically have five to eight stakeholders involved in the buying decision. Reaching one of them with the same message you sent to 2,000 others isn’t a strategy. It’s a coin toss on your most important deals.

### What Account-Based Marketing Actually Means

Account-based marketing flips the usual logic. Instead of starting with a broad market and hoping the right companies respond, you start with a named list of accounts you specifically want to win, then build coordinated campaigns around each one.

The goal is simple: by the time your account executive reaches out, several people inside that account already recognise your name.

### Tier Your Accounts Before You Touch Them

Not every target account deserves the same effort. Split your list into tiers:

1. Tier 1: your highest-value targets. They get the most research, the most personalised messaging, and coordinated outreach to multiple stakeholders.
2. Tier 2: strong-fit accounts with solid deal potential. They get tailored angles by segment, with lighter personalisation.
3. Tier 3: good-fit accounts that stay on a lighter-touch cadence until they show a buying signal.

Most ABM programs run somewhere between 50 and 250 named accounts. The right number depends on one question: how many engaged accounts can your sales team actually follow up on properly?

### Coordinate the Channels, Don’t Just Stack Them

Running email and LinkedIn separately against the same accounts is not ABM. It’s two disconnected campaigns that happen to overlap.

In a real ABM motion, the channels reinforce each other. The finance head sees your LinkedIn post, the operations lead gets an email about a problem specific to their role, and the founder receives a connection request referencing the same theme. Every touch tells one consistent story.

### The Handoff Is Where ABM Pays Off

Most ABM programs fall apart at the last step. Marketing generates engagement, then sales opens with a cold introduction as if nothing happened.

Track engagement at the account level, not just the contact level: who was contacted, on which channel, what they replied to, and how warm the account is overall. When your AE walks into the first call knowing which stakeholders are engaged and why, the conversation starts in the middle instead of the beginning. Across our ABM work, engaged accounts have produced a 6.4x higher average deal size.

### When ABM Is the Wrong Choice

## ABM isn’t for everyone. If you don’t have a finite list of named accounts, or your average deal size doesn’t justify deep account-level personalisation, a broad cold email or LinkedIn program will serve you better. ABM is a precision tool. Use it where precision pays.

1. Beyond the Trade Fair: How Indian Manufacturers and Exporters Can Find Global Buyers Through Outbound
URL: [https://www.myntmore.com/blog/outbound-for-manufacturers-exporters](https://www.myntmore.com/blog/outbound-for-manufacturers-exporters)

₹8 to 15 lakh for a booth. Three days of handshakes. Two leads who stop replying. For many Indian exporters, the trade fair is still the default sales strategy, and it’s quietly the most expensive one.

### The Export Sales Model Hasn’t Kept Up

Ask most Indian manufacturers how they find international buyers and you’ll hear two answers: trade fairs and B2B portals. Both made sense for a long time. Neither is enough anymore.

Trade fairs are slow and costly. One deal can take six months of follow-up after the event. Portals like IndiaMART and Alibaba bring volume, but much of it is price shoppers, and your sales team ends up sorting hundreds of low-quality enquiries to find a handful of serious buyers.

### The Real Problem: You Don’t Know Who to Target

The biggest barrier for exporters isn’t the product. Indian manufacturers often have the capability, the certifications, and the pricing to compete globally. The barrier is knowing exactly who to talk to in a new market: which importer, which distributor, which procurement head has the product fit and buying capacity.

Outbound solves that problem directly. Instead of waiting for buyers to find you at a booth or on a portal, you identify them and reach out first.

### A Simple Outbound System for Exporters

1. Map the buyer, not just the market: define product category, buyer type (importer, distributor, or OEM), country, company size, and how procurement works there.
2. Build a verified prospect list: target named procurement heads, sourcing managers, and importers. Old trade directories go stale fast; verified contacts don’t.
3. Localise the message: tone and buying behaviour differ between Germany, the UAE, and Southeast Asia. One template for every market reads like spam everywhere.
4. Lead with credibility: put ISO certifications, compliance credentials, and existing international clients front and centre. Overseas buyers are asking one question first: can this supplier deliver reliably?
5. Follow up with substance: spec sheets, product samples, and buyer testimonials move serious buyers forward. “Just checking in” doesn’t.

### What This Looks Like in Practice

An ISO-certified auto components manufacturer in Gujarat had relied entirely on trade fairs and portals. A targeted cold email and LinkedIn campaign aimed at procurement managers at Tier-1 auto manufacturers in Europe and Southeast Asia produced 14 qualified international buyer meetings in one quarter, with three leading to formal RFQs.

A Surat textile manufacturer wanted to enter the Middle East and Africa without funding trade delegations. Personalised outreach to importers and distributors across the UAE, Kenya, and Nigeria led to 11 distributor conversations and three signed partnership agreements within 90 days.

### Keep the Fair, Change the Strategy

## This doesn’t mean trade fairs are useless. They work best when buyers already know your name before they reach your booth. Run outbound in the months before an event, book meetings ahead of time, and turn an expensive gamble into a scheduled set of conversations. The exporters winning new markets today aren’t waiting to be found. They’re reaching the right buyer first.

1. The Network You Forgot: Why Your Existing Connections Are Your Cheapest Pipeline
URL: [https://www.myntmore.com/blog/existing-connection-nurturing](https://www.myntmore.com/blog/existing-connection-nurturing)

You have hundreds, maybe thousands, of LinkedIn connections. Former colleagues, past clients, people you met at events. Most of them haven’t heard from you in years. That’s pipeline sitting idle.

### Every Founder Is Sitting on a Dormant Network

Founders spend serious money building cold lists from scratch: data tools, enrichment, verification, sending infrastructure. Meanwhile, the network they’ve already built goes completely quiet.

Those connections cost nothing to acquire. Each one is someone who already accepted your request or once worked with you. But a relationship you never touch slowly turns back into a stranger. By the time you finally reach out with an offer, the message feels just as cold as one to someone you’ve never met.

### Familiar Beats Cold, Every Time

The difference between a cold message and a warm one is rarely the wording. It’s recognition. When someone sees your name and remembers your last comment on their post, your message lands as a continuation, not an interruption.

That’s the idea behind existing connection nurturing: light, ongoing engagement with people already in your network, so that when a real outreach message arrives, it feels familiar rather than cold.

### Nurture Without Selling

The key is consistency without pressure. A simple rhythm works:

1. Engage before you message: like and leave a genuine comment on a connection’s post. Thoughtful comments get noticed far more than likes.
2. Check in with no ask: every few months, send a short note about something real. A promotion, a company milestone, a post that made you think.
3. Share something useful: a relevant article, benchmark, or template with one line on why you thought of them.
4. Watch for signals: a new role, a funding round, or a hiring push often means fresh priorities. That’s when a real conversation becomes relevant.

### Where to Start

Don’t try to nurture your entire network at once. Start with three groups:

1. Former clients: they know your work and have already trusted you once.
2. Past colleagues now in decision-making roles: people who moved up often control budgets today.
3. Warm conversations that went quiet: prospects who engaged but never moved forward.

Pick 20 to 30 people from these groups and spend 15 minutes a day engaging with them. It’s a small habit that compounds quickly.

### Don’t Make It Look Scripted

The fastest way to ruin nurturing is to automate it badly. Copy-paste birthday messages, generic “great post!” comments, and an obvious pitch three days after reconnecting all signal that you only showed up to sell. If a message could be sent to anyone, don’t send it.

### Your Network Is an Asset, If You Maintain It

Cold outbound builds new relationships. Nurturing protects the ones you already have. The founders with the most reliable pipelines do both: they reach new prospects consistently and stay present with the people who already know them. Before you buy another list, look at the one you already own.
